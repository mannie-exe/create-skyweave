# Encounters and settlement population

Encounter additions retain native combat and AI. Movement additions must preserve readable animations and ordinary access before adding optional traversal challenges. [Movement and combat](../players/movement-combat.md) owns player controls; [structure policy](structures.md#settlement-encounter-policy) owns village opportunities and fitting. Exact dependency pins live in `empack.yml` and `empack.lock`.

## Guard population and village size

Guard Villagers 2.4.12 injects after `SinglePoolElement.place`. Its configured template list initially contains only `minecraft:village/common/iron_golem`; the number labelled “guards in a village” is actually requested per matching template invocation. A village with two markers can request twelve guards in one pass, while a village with no marker requests none. Sky Villages use their own templates and have no default matching marker.

The selected hook does not independently check the placement return value, chunk clipping or once-only realization. Do not add large house or town-center templates to the list as a quick coverage fix. Repeated piece invocations or relocated saved aliases must be qualified before predicting a population from marker counts. The existing physical-layout identity in Compat and discovery deduplication do not certify once-only entity spawning.

Recruitment is another native route: crouch and use an accepted sword or crossbow on an adult unemployed villager or nitwit. Hero of the Village is not required for conversion by default. Following does require that effect, inventory access requires reputation 15, and following guards may teleport. Clerics and smiths provide native healing and repairs with three daily uses. Native guard arrows can damage villagers, golems and other guards; equipment drops are 100%, equipment durability-loss chance is 1%, and raid illagers can attack animals. Treat these as explicit balance choices rather than assuming the mod only adds cosmetic defenders.

There is no native guard-to-bed or guard-to-resident ratio. Keep iron golems: the intended direction is a shared defender placement policy, not blanket removal of their templates or native spawning. Provisional owner preferences are one guard to two golems for a small settlement, and two guards to one or two golems for a medium settlement. These are design targets, not configured defaults; village size/population semantics and larger-settlement rules remain unsettled.

Prefer a bounded, once-per-physical-layout initial allocation from retained inhabited pieces or actual supported population markers. Use safe local spawn positions and preserve recruitment. Keep authored initial golems separate from later native villager-driven spawning and player-built golems; none is implicitly disabled by a guard allocation. Do not base an every-tick population loop on all beds or force extra village candidacy. Ruined settlements and Sky Villages need explicit policies. Resource-aware scaling, including a golem-only fallback without Guard Villagers, belongs to the separate Skyweave mod's future settlement design rather than this pack's current placement pass.

Village sizes use several independent controls. The authored CTOV selection weights are `small:medium:large = 10:4:1`; an eligible regional T&T choice has weight 8. These are conditional weights, not guaranteed global percentages. Native jigsaw depth and pools determine assembly, and Compat can reject or trim unsupported layouts. T&T's birch village uses depth 6 and maximum distance 116; its native house pool already contains medium and large houses. Sparse-looking streets alone do not prove that its house pool is missing. T&T forest villages are intentional ruins and should not be counted as populated communities for guard allocation.

For a saved-world census, count valid start records and physical layouts separately. Ignore reference-only entries; normalize native jigsaw assembly-only bounding-box padding consistently with Compat's `PhysicalStructureIdentity`. Report the actual saved coverage and status, chosen horizontal boundary, distinct ground/Sky Villages and retained piece composition. Associate saved residents and home POIs in three dimensions without double counting nearby or stacked settlements. These counts describe saved state, not accessible housing, remaining spawn capacity, or the fraction of eligible natural terrain. Never derive a village spawn percentage by dividing by surrounding void chunks.

## Biome variants and island altitude

Gelids replace zombies in cold-biome tags and inflict freezing. Thickets replace jungle zombies and inflict poison; Verdants replace jungle skeletons and fire poison arrows. The improved Stray, Husk and Bogged selections also replace eligible vanilla entities. In the selected binary, ordinary replacement accepts natural, chunk-generation and structure spawn reasons, checks the actual biome at the entity's physical position, and rolls the configured 30% chance. This is conditional on an eligible base spawn, not 30% of the monster cap; native biome spawn tables can also supply Strays, Husks and Bogged independently. Ordinary block spawners use the separate 5% per-emission profile below; trial chambers retain their own switches and alias selections.

The managed encounter profile selects `PER_EMISSION`: after choosing the lowest-priority enabled matching biome mapping, one 5% roll applies to each eligible zombie/skeleton block-spawner attempt. It uses the emitted position's native seeded 3D biome lookup over existing chunks. Overlapping tags never add independent rolls. Native light, obstruction, initialization, equipment, delay and activation remain; mixed base/variant identities share the native nearby cap. Names and applicable NBT survive, while stored `SpawnData`, `SpawnPotentials` and the preview are unchanged. Failed emissions do not reroll or insert a fallback. Trial spawners, minecarts, eggs, passengers and authored non-base types retain native behavior.

All six native `*SpawnerChance` values are explicitly zero, preventing generation-time conversion from stacking with per-emission selection. Compat refuses startup if per-emission mode is combined with nonzero native dungeon chances. To restore native generation-time conversion, select `NATIVE_TYPE`, deliberately set the desired native chances and restart. Native conversion chooses a stored type once in `MonsterRoomFeature`; it does not roll each emission. Existing authored variant spawners remain variants and are not automatically migrated.

Trial chambers use separate enabled switches. Against the pinned native alias lists, Gelid and Thicket extend melee choices from zombie/husk/spider to five equally weighted choices; Murk and Verdant extend the paired ranged/slow-ranged choices from skeleton/stray/bogged to five equally weighted groups. Each choice is therefore 20% in its family before other modifications. Alias choices apply across a generated structure, not independently to each mob emission. Small-melee choices and Breeze content remain unchanged. These additions do not use ordinary dungeon or per-emission chances or require the corresponding surface biome; trial Murks bypass the natural ocean-depth predicate.

The four supplied trial-spawner templates configure 20 ticks between successful spawn opportunities, three simultaneous mobs plus 0.5 per additional player, and inherited total-mob defaults of six plus two per additional player. Native calculations floor the resulting targets. Their ominous configurations add the native melee/ranged trial equipment tables with zero equipment-drop chance, and ominous reward-table weights of three for keys and seven for consumables. Native activation, collision/line-of-sight checks, player scaling, cooldown and rewards still govern encounters. These are template/source findings, not full-pack combat qualification.

Resource Patches repairs the selected 1.0.29 archive's two missing slow-ranged references. The `minecraft:trial_chambers/spawner/slow_ranged/{murk,verdant}` pools reuse V&V's existing ranged templates with the pack-owned `create_skyweave:variants_slow_ranged` processor. Its native `append_static` modifier merges only timing/scaling into normal and ominous configurations: 160 ticks between opportunities, four simultaneous mobs and two more per additional player, matching native slow-ranged templates. Native block-entity loading merges normal into ominous; both V&V configurations are explicitly corrected because both supplied 20-tick values. Total-mob scaling, variant IDs, equipment and rewards remain intact. Ranged and melee templates keep their existing cadence.

The overrides and processor require V&V through native NeoForge resource conditions. They retain pool weight one, rigid projection, empty fallback and the existing family aliases. No third-party NBT is redistributed or edited. On a V&V/Minecraft update, inspect the effective template closure and actual normal/ominous decoded configurations; retire redundant overrides only after the upstream repair is qualified. Resource repair is independent of Compat's optional spawner mode. With Lithostitched 1.8.1, Compat's independent `variantsandventures.lithostitched_trials` bridge also restores these additions after Lithostitched replaces the native alias types. It runs after that modifier pass and before level creation, preserves native selection/RNG while normalizing the two extension slots, then replays V&V's original updater and its enable switches. Small-melee and Breeze content remain unchanged. Restart the server after changing this configuration or its alias resources; hot reload is not supported. Existing generated trial chambers and stored spawner identities are not migrated.

The six minimum-height settings are absolute lower bounds. Using -128 removes the old -64 cutoff; it does not remap biome selection or prove spawning in every overlapping island layer. The selected in-game controls allow this value. Verify server ownership, actual stored/queried 3D biome tags and the unchanged native light/fluid rules when changing world generation.

Murk is an additive monster-category underwater skeleton with weight 4 and one-member groups in warm, lukewarm and deep lukewarm oceans. Weight is relative, not a percentage. Its natural predicate requires water below, a strict `Y < water datum - 5` depth and the existing one-in-forty roll, with no added darkness rule. Compat's independent `variantsandventures.local_water` adapter supplies the current connected loaded column datum only in the active island preset. Last-water Y plus one preserves the native boundary, including covered water and separate layers sharing X/Z. Its swimming goal re-queries that local layer as it moves. Missing/dry/over-budget columns refuse without generating terrain or planning watersheds. Foreign worlds retain global sea level, and non-natural/trial reasons retain their native path. These correctness changes do not increase weight or group size.

The selected Gelid, Thicket and Verdant replacement handlers combine the entity-enable and spawn-enable flags with `OR`. Turning only the spawn-enable flag off is not a reliable replacement hold; a zero replacement chance provides a direct control for that path. Check spawner and entity-registration paths separately. Preserve original entity state and qualify replacement failure before adding a runtime adaptation.

## Illagers, raids and structures

Illager Invasion expands familiar raids with ranged, shield, support and spellcasting roles. Its configured Invoker stays out of ordinary raids; it can still be encountered through other routes. The native Imbuing Table, enchantment upgrades, platinum trim effects and other equipment/rewards are gameplay additions requiring separate balance review. They are not removed by disabling raid participation.

The selected patrol mixin replaces each newly created patrol member with a Marauder on a 50% roll. Raid participation switches do not disable this patrol path or authored structure residents. There is no single native switch for all three systems. Disabling vanilla patrol spawning is broader than suppressing only Marauders.

The pinned vanilla patrol scheduler attempts every 12,000 to 13,199 ticks, approximately 10 to 11 minutes at 20 TPS. It requires at least five calendar days elapsed, daytime and a one-in-five roll, then chooses one player server-side. Spectators and players within the native two-section village-proximity check are rejected. A candidate offsets each horizontal axis by 24 to 47 blocks, requires the surrounding chunks already present, rejects `without_patrol_spawns` biomes, and uses the top motion-blocking non-leaf height. Physical space, supporting block, non-Peaceful difficulty and block light at most 8 must pass; group attempts are `ceil(effective local difficulty) + 1`, with the first member as leader. This custom-spawner path is separate from the ordinary monster-cap selection. Void, stacked top heightmaps and missing terrain can reject island candidates. The pack’s independent Compat control replaces only the 20% probability gate with 15%; standalone Compat defaults to the exact native 20% RNG path. The interval, five-day/daytime rules and Illager Invasion’s separate Marauder substitution remain native. This percentage is not a realized per-player patrol rate.

Village raids retain the native 1.21.1 trigger: drink an ominous bottle for Bad Omen, enter a logical village to gain 30 seconds of Raid Omen, then create or extend a raid at the stored position. A logical village uses occupied village POIs, not just a structure ID. Vanilla raiders remain and Illager Invasion augments the wave roster. Patrols and the five authored enemy structures provide fights outside villages without creating an independent wave-based invasion timer or requiring Bad Omen.

Structure rarity is data-driven, not controlled by the raid TOML:

| Firecaller hut | Badlands | Spacing 42, separation 28 | Jigsaw depth 3; surface projection |
| Sorcerer hut | Dark forest | Spacing 18, separation 14 | Jigsaw depth 2; surface projection |
| Illusioner tower | Taiga, old-growth pine/spruce taiga, dark forest, swamp | Spacing 42, separation 28 | Depth 2; village-set exclusion within 10 chunks |

Spacing describes candidate grids before biome, frequency and fit losses. The fort's 0.2 is a placement-frequency control, not a per-island chance. The pack's effective `minecraft:villages` set contains curated CTOV/T&T choices; verify exclusion against its effective placement behavior rather than assuming a separate unmodified vanilla village set. Native exclusion checks the other set's placement candidates, not successful saved village starts, so rejection can survive later biome or fitting losses. Unknown foreign sets do not automatically join Compat's managed village or outpost quotas. Review exact category, host selection, projection and support before adding family tags. In particular, a large underground labyrinth needs an island-body/depth review rather than a generic surface-outpost classification. Natural Skyweave placement remains unqualified for these five additions.

Before biome, exclusion and fitting, ordinary random-spread candidate density is approximately inverse spacing squared. Against the village data's spacing 34, spacing 42 is about 0.66 times its raw grid density, spacing 18 about 3.57 times, and spacing 48 about 0.50 times; the fort's frequency further reduces its raw figure to about 0.13 times. These are grid comparisons, not extrapolated realized counts from the 22 saved ground villages: Compat's village host, quota and layout policies differ. A future placement review must count each rejection stage and actual retained starts over the same saved/generation coverage before tuning rarity.

## Disabled and held directions

Hostile Skies' automatic tick path returns when `enableRaidSpawns=false`. Keeping it off does not remove operator commands, ship capture or other content. Permission-level-2 commands include `/hostileskies ships`, `/hostileskies spawnraid [ship] [omen]` and `/hostileskies stopraid`; manual spawning does not use the automatic-enable gate.

The selected native controls permit 1 to 120 minutes between attempts and 1 to 20 active raids. The staged owner-selected rules are 90 minutes between attempts, starting chance 0%, a 33.333333333333336-percentage-point increase after failed eligible rolls, maximum 100%, and eight active raids server-wide. Automatic spawning remains off. With a fresh/reset chance and no blocked attempts, rolls occur at elapsed hours `1.5:0%, 3:33⅓%, 4.5:66⅔%, 6:100%`. A successful spawn resets the chance. This uses server game time, not each player's active hours; a capacity or eligibility block adds the separate native one percentage point. A 100% random roll does not override unavailable ships, valid placement, player eligibility or capacity handling. Stored chance and last-attempt time survive restarts; changing the base does not erase an existing saved chance.

Native personal cooldown remains ten minutes and nearby eligible players group within 200 horizontal blocks. Creative/spectator players, players on cooldown and players already targeted by an active raid are ineligible. Each automatic attempt selects one group; increasing the cap does not spawn eight ships at once. Tier weighting, unlocks, Bad Omen scaling and encounter departure are separate rules; do not enable this as a side effect of installing new illagers.

BK's Invaders is held for review, not installed. The reviewed 2.0.2 artifact requires EF and four additional mods: Armaments 4.5.11, Armor 3.0.1, Weapon Skills 2.0.13 and Poise & Stance 4.2.7. That stack adds gear, skills, rendering hooks and a global stagger/stamina/guard/dodge overhaul. Poise & Stance also defaults to replacing PC4's stamina provider and can restore tracked configuration defaults on updates; merely disabling ambient invasions would not neutralize those dependencies.

Invaders separates random attacks, structure-entry encounters, authored residents, manual summons/cooperators and an arena. Its `ambientInvasions` control gates random and structure-entry encounters, while resident/reset sites remain separate. The native site system can restore entire captured block/block-entity areas as well as residents or loot; a future trial must explicitly settle player edits, loot renewal and Lootr interaction. Do not treat it as an EF-only enemy pack or install its stack transitively while it is held. Mowzie's remains deferred.

## Integration and dependency updates

When updating these dependencies:

1. Resolve and pin exact native artifacts with empack, then review loader metadata, required libraries and both installation sides. Check equivalent selections across provider catalogs to avoid duplicate JARs.
2. Compare native config readers, server/client ownership, startup registration and runtime consumers. Generate canonical configs in an isolated server, reapply deliberate rules and verify save/reopen bytes. Review managed-file digests and bindings together.
3. Compare biome tags, spawn reasons, absolute-height/sea-level rules, raid wave composition, patrol substitution, structure sets/pools/processors and authored inhabitants. Config loading alone does not prove correct natural spawning.
4. Qualify entity AI/ability/animation transitions with the selected movement stack in an isolated actual-pack client/server. Include cliffs, water, doors, friendly fire, recruiting/following, death/reconnect and unsupported/native fallback. Ship navigation and parkour-capable NPC behavior remain distinct work.
5. Build and inspect all exports; verify common JAR/config inclusion and the absence of held dependencies. Recheck retained village fitting and saved generation identity before any separately authorized rollout. Changing rules does not authorize a world reset.
